# Indian Clinical Laboratory Biological Reference Intervals & Panic Values (ICMR / AIIMS Standards)

**Document Type:** Clinical Laboratory Diagnostic Standards Manual  
**Domain:** laboratory  
**Reference Standard:** AIIMS New Delhi, ICMR National Population Guidelines & NABL Diagnostic Intervals

## 1. Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Hematology Profile

| Parameter / Analyte | Conventional Units | Adult Reference Interval (Male) | Adult Reference Interval (Female) | Critical / Panic Values | Clinical Significance of Abnormalities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hemoglobin (Hb)** | g/dL | 13.5 – 17.5 g/dL | 12.0 – 15.5 g/dL (Pregnancy: 11.0 – 14.0) | < 7.0 g/dL (Transfusion Trigger) or > 20.0 g/dL | Anemia grading: Mild (10–11.9), Moderate (8–9.9), Severe (< 8.0 g/dL). Microcytic indicates Iron Deficiency or Thalassemia; Macrocytic indicates B12/Folate deficiency. |
| **Total Leukocyte Count (TLC / WBC)** | /µL | 4,000 – 11,000 /µL | 4,000 – 11,000 /µL | < 2,000 /µL (Agranulocytosis risk) or > 30,000 /µL | Leukocytosis indicates acute bacterial infection, severe tissue inflammation, or leukemia. Leukopenia indicates viral infections (Dengue, Typhoid), sepsis, or bone marrow suppression. |
| **Neutrophils (Absolute & %)** | % (cells/µL) | 40% – 70% (1,500 – 7,500 /µL) | 40% – 70% (1,500 – 7,500 /µL) | ANC < 500 /µL (Critical infection vulnerability) | Neutrophilia with toxic granulations indicates pyogenic bacterial infection. Severe neutropenia requires immediate protective isolation. |
| **Lymphocytes** | % (cells/µL) | 20% – 40% (1,000 – 4,000 /µL) | 20% – 40% (1,000 – 4,000 /µL) | < 800 /µL (Lymphopenia) | Lymphocytosis indicates viral infection (Infectious Mononucleosis, CMV, viral hepatitis), tuberculosis, or chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). |
| **Eosinophils** | % (cells/µL) | 1% – 6% (50 – 500 /µL) | 1% – 6% (50 – 500 /µL) | > 1,500 /µL (Hypereosinophilic Syndrome) | Eosinophilia indicates allergic rhinitis, bronchial asthma, drug hypersensitivity, or parasitic/helminthic worm infestations. |
| **Monocytes** | % (cells/µL) | 2% – 8% (200 – 800 /µL) | 2% – 8% (200 – 800 /µL) | > 1,000 /µL | Monocytosis occurs in chronic bacterial infections, tuberculosis, subacute bacterial endocarditis, and recovery from bone marrow suppression. |
| **Platelet Count** | Lakhs/µL (/µL) | 1.5 – 4.5 Lakhs/µL (150,000 – 450,000 /µL) | 1.5 – 4.5 Lakhs/µL (150,000 – 450,000 /µL) | < 20,000 /µL (Spontaneous Hemorrhage risk) or > 10 Lakhs/µL | Thrombocytopenia grading: Grade 1 (75k-150k), Grade 2 (50k-75k), Grade 3 (20k-50k), Grade 4 (<20k /µL). Monitored daily in Dengue viral fever, ITP, and sepsis. |
| **Packed Cell Volume (PCV / Hematocrit)** | % | 40.0% – 50.0% | 36.0% – 46.0% | < 20.0% or > 60.0% | Evaluates hemoconcentration in severe dehydration, burns, and Dengue Shock Syndrome (hematocrit rise > 20% indicates vascular plasma leak). |
| **Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR)** | mm/1st hr | 0 – 15 mm/hr | 0 – 20 mm/hr | > 100 mm/hr | Nonspecific inflammatory marker. Extreme elevation (>100 mm/hr) strongly points toward Multiple Myeloma, Temporal Arteritis, or active Tuberculosis. |

## 2. Kidney Function Tests (KFT / RFT) & Electrolytes

| Parameter | Units | Standard Adult Reference Range | Critical / Panic Values | Clinical Interpretation & Diagnostic Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Serum Creatinine** | mg/dL | Male: 0.7 – 1.3 mg/dL<br>Female: 0.6 – 1.1 mg/dL | > 5.0 mg/dL (Severe Acute Kidney Injury) | Glomerular filtration biomarker. Elevated in AKI, Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), urinary tract obstruction, and hypovolemic dehydration. Calculate eGFR. |
| **Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)** | mg/dL | 7.0 – 20.0 mg/dL (Blood Urea: 15 – 40 mg/dL) | > 80 mg/dL (Severe Uremic Encephalopathy) | Elevated in prerenal azotemia (upper GI bleeding, heart failure, dehydration). BUN:Creatinine ratio > 20:1 indicates prerenal etiology. |
| **Serum Uric Acid** | mg/dL | Male: 3.5 – 7.2 mg/dL<br>Female: 2.6 – 6.0 mg/dL | > 12.0 mg/dL | Hyperuricemia (>7.0 mg/dL) precipitates acute gouty arthritis and uric acid nephrolithiasis. |
| **Serum Sodium (Na+)** | mEq/L (mmol/L) | 135 – 145 mEq/L | < 120 mEq/L (Seizure Risk) or > 160 mEq/L | Hyponatremia (<135) causes confusion, cerebral edema, seizures; overcorrection risk of Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome. Hypernatremia causes severe intracellular dehydration. |
| **Serum Potassium (K+)** | mEq/L (mmol/L) | 3.5 – 5.0 mEq/L | < 2.8 mEq/L (Ventricular Arrhythmia) or > 6.2 mEq/L (Cardiac Arrest) | Hypokalemia causes flat T waves, U waves, and paralytic ileus. Hyperkalemia causes tall peaked T waves, QRS widening, and fatal ventricular asystole. |
| **Serum Chloride (Cl-)** | mEq/L (mmol/L) | 96 – 106 mEq/L | < 80 or > 125 mEq/L | Used to compute Serum Anion Gap = [Na+] - ([Cl-] + [HCO3-]). Normal gap: 8–12 mEq/L. High gap indicates DKA, lactic acidosis, or uremia. |
| **Serum Calcium (Total)** | mg/dL | 8.5 – 10.5 mg/dL | < 6.5 mg/dL (Tetany & Laryngospasm) or > 13.0 mg/dL | Corrected Calcium = Measured Total Calcium + 0.8 * (4.0 - Albumin). Hypocalcemia causes Chvostek's/Trousseau's signs; hypercalcemia suggests hyperparathyroidism or malignancy. |

## 3. Liver Function Tests (LFT) & Hepatic Enzyme Panel

| Analyte / Enzyme | Units | Standard Biological Reference Interval | Clinical Significance of Elevations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Bilirubin** | mg/dL | 0.2 – 1.2 mg/dL | Jaundice clinically visible when total bilirubin exceeds 2.5 mg/dL. Evaluates biliary clearance and hemolysis. |
| **Direct (Conjugated) Bilirubin** | mg/dL | 0.0 – 0.3 mg/dL | Direct fraction > 50% indicates cholestasis, bile duct obstruction (calculi, stricture), or intrahepatic cholestasis. |
| **Indirect (Unconjugated) Bilirubin** | mg/dL | 0.2 – 0.8 mg/dL | Elevated in intravascular/extravascular hemolysis (hemolytic anemia) or genetic Gilbert's syndrome. |
| **SGPT / ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)** | U/L | 7 – 56 U/L | Highly specific for hepatocellular injury. Extreme elevations (>1,000 U/L) seen in acute viral hepatitis (A, B, E), paracetamol overdose, and hepatic ischemia. |
| **SGOT / AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase)** | U/L | 10 – 40 U/L | Present in liver, cardiac, and skeletal muscle. AST:ALT ratio > 2.0 strongly indicates alcoholic liver disease. ALT > AST typical of NAFLD / NASH. |
| **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)** | U/L | 44 – 147 U/L | Marker of biliary ductal epithelium injury and bone turnover. Elevated in gallstones, biliary obstruction, bone metastasis, and healing fractures. |
| **Serum Albumin** | g/dL | 3.5 – 5.0 g/dL | Solely synthesized by hepatocytes (half-life 20 days). Marker of chronic liver synthetic capacity. Hypoalbuminemia (<3.5) causes ascites and pedal edema. |
| **Total Protein & A/G Ratio** | g/dL | 6.0 – 8.3 g/dL (A/G Ratio: 1.2 – 2.2) | Reversed A/G ratio (<1.0) seen in chronic cirrhosis, autoimmune chronic active hepatitis, and Multiple Myeloma. |

## 4. Glycemic Control, Metabolic Panel & Lipid Profile

| Test | Units | Normal / Desirable Values | Borderline / Prediabetes | Diagnostic Cutoff for Disease |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG)** | mg/dL | 70 – 99 mg/dL | 100 – 125 mg/dL (Impaired Fasting) | >= 126 mg/dL (Diabetes Mellitus on 2 separate occasions) |
| **Postprandial Glucose (PPBG - 2 hr)** | mg/dL | < 140 mg/dL | 140 – 199 mg/dL (Impaired Glucose Tolerance) | >= 200 mg/dL (Diabetes Mellitus) |
| **Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c)** | % | < 5.7% | 5.7% – 6.4% (High Risk Prediabetes) | >= 6.5% (Diabetes Mellitus). Adult treatment goal is typically < 7.0%. |
| **Total Cholesterol** | mg/dL | < 200 mg/dL | 200 – 239 mg/dL | >= 240 mg/dL (Hypercholesterolemia) |
| **Serum Triglycerides** | mg/dL | < 150 mg/dL | 150 – 199 mg/dL | 200 – 499 mg/dL (High); >= 500 mg/dL (Imminent Acute Pancreatitis Risk) |
| **HDL Cholesterol ("Good")** | mg/dL | Male: > 40 mg/dL<br>Female: > 50 mg/dL | — | < 40 mg/dL confers significant atherosclerotic risk. |
| **LDL Cholesterol ("Bad")** | mg/dL | < 100 mg/dL (< 70 for CAD/Diabetics) | 100 – 159 mg/dL | >= 160 mg/dL (High); >= 190 mg/dL (Severe Primary Hypercholesterolemia) |
| **Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)** | µIU/mL | 0.4 – 4.5 µIU/mL | 4.5 – 10.0 µIU/mL (Subclinical Hypothyroidism) | > 10.0 µIU/mL (Overt Hypothyroidism); < 0.1 µIU/mL (Hyperthyroidism / Thyrotoxicosis) |
| **Free T4 (FT4)** | ng/dL | 0.8 – 1.8 ng/dL | — | Low in overt primary hypothyroidism; elevated in Graves' hyperthyroidism. |
| **Serum Ferritin** | ng/mL | Male: 20 – 250 ng/mL<br>Female: 10 – 120 ng/mL | — | < 15 ng/mL is diagnostic of Iron Deficiency Anemia. > 1,000 ng/mL seen in hemochromatosis. |
| **Vitamin B12** | pg/mL | 200 – 900 pg/mL | 200 – 300 pg/mL | < 200 pg/mL (Deficiency: Megaloblastic anemia and peripheral sensory neuropathy). |
| **25-Hydroxy Vitamin D3** | ng/mL | 30.0 – 100.0 ng/mL | 20.0 – 29.9 ng/mL (Insufficiency) | < 20.0 ng/mL (Deficiency: Requires oral Cholecalciferol 60,000 IU weekly for 8 weeks). |

## 5. Routine Urinalysis & Microscopic Examination

| Urine Test Parameter | Standard Normal Value | Abnormal Finding & Diagnostic Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Color & Appearance** | Pale yellow, clear | Red/smoky indicates hematuria or myoglobinuria; cloudy/turbid indicates pyuria or high phosphate crystals. |
| **Specific Gravity** | 1.005 – 1.030 | Fixed at 1.010 (isosthenuria) indicates end-stage chronic kidney disease (loss of tubular concentrating ability). |
| **pH** | 4.5 – 8.0 (Average 6.0) | Alkaline pH (>7.5) suggests infection by urea-splitting organisms (Proteus mirabilis, triple phosphate stones). |
| **Protein / Albumin** | Negative (or < 150 mg/day) | Trace to 4+ indicates glomerular injury (nephrotic syndrome, diabetic nephropathy, glomerulonephritis). |
| **Urine Glucose** | Negative | Positive when serum glucose exceeds renal tubular absorptive threshold (> 180 mg/dL). |
| **Urine Ketones** | Negative | Positive indicates Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA), prolonged fasting/starvation ketosis, or severe vomiting. |
| **Pus Cells (Leukocytes / WBC)** | 0 – 5 /HPF | > 5 /HPF (Pyuria) confirms active Urinary Tract Infection (UTI), urethritis, or cystitis. |
| **Red Blood Cells (RBCs)** | 0 – 2 /HPF | > 3 /HPF (Microscopic Hematuria) indicates urinary tract calculus (stone), glomerulonephritis, or malignancy. |
| **Epithelial Cells** | 0 – 5 /HPF | Excessive squamous epithelial cells indicate external skin/perineal contamination of the midstream urine sample. |
| **Casts** | Occasional Hyaline casts normal | RBC casts indicate Glomerulonephritis; WBC casts indicate Pyelonephritis; Muddy brown granular casts indicate Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN). |
