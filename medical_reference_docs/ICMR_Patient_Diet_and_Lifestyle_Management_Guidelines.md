# ICMR & NIN Patient Diet and Lifestyle Management Guidelines

**Document Type:** Patient Nutrition, Dietary Modification & Disease Management Manual  
**Domain:** patient_diet_and_lifestyle  
**Target Audience:** Patients with Metabolic Conditions, Hypertension, Fatty Liver & Gout  
**Clinical Standards:** ICMR, National Institute of Nutrition (NIN) & RSSDI Aligned  

---

## 1. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus & Pre-Diabetes Nutrition

### Understanding Glycemic Index (GI) in Indian Diets
* Carbohydrates that break down rapidly into glucose have a **High Glycemic Index (> 70)**, causing sudden blood sugar spikes. 
* Foods that digest slowly have a **Low Glycemic Index (< 55)**, keeping blood sugar stable and reducing insulin demand.

### High GI Foods to Avoid or Strictly Limit
* **Refined Flours & Grains:** Maida, naan, bhature, white bread, polished white rice, instant noodles, suji/rava in large quantities.
* **Starchy Root Vegetables:** Deep-fried potatoes, aloo parathas with butter, sweet potato.
* **High-Sugar Fruits:** Mangoes, chiku (sapodilla), grapes, banana, watermelon in large quantities.
* **Liquid Sugars:** Canned fruit juices (even 'no added sugar' versions lack fiber and cause rapid sugar spikes), sweetened carbonated sodas, packaged lassi.
* **Indian Sweets & Bakery:** Mithai, jalebi, gulab jamun, biscuits, rusks, cakes.

### Low to Medium GI Indian Foods to Choose
* **Whole Grains & Millets:** Bajra (pearl millet), Jowar (sorghum), Ragi (finger millet in moderation), whole wheat flour with multi-grain bran, steel-cut oats.
* **Whole Pulses & Legumes:** Whole moong, kala chana, rajma, sprouted grains, besan (chickpea flour) cheela.
* **Low-Sugar High-Fiber Fruits:** Guava (amrood), green apples, jamun (Indian blackberry), papaya (1 small bowl), pomegranate, amla (Indian gooseberry).
* **Vegetables:** Bitter gourd (karela), fenugreek (methi) leaves, spinach, bottle gourd (lauki), ridge gourd (torai), cucumber, tomatoes, bell peppers.

### The "Quarter-Plate" Meal Model for Indian Diets
* **50% of the Plate (Half):** Non-starchy cooked green vegetables and fresh raw salad (cucumber, tomato, radish, leafy greens).
* **25% of the Plate (One Quarter):** Quality protein (thick dal, sprouted lentils, boiled egg whites, grilled paneer, or tofu).
* **25% of the Plate (One Quarter):** High-fiber complex carbohydrates (1 to 2 small whole-wheat or multi-grain rotis, or small portion of brown rice).

## 2. High Uric Acid & Gout (Hyperuricemia) Dietary Protocol

### How Diet Affects Uric Acid
* Uric acid is created when the body breaks down chemical compounds called **purines**.
* When uric acid levels exceed 7.0 mg/dL in men or 6.0 mg/dL in women, sharp needle-like monosodium urate crystals can deposit into joints (frequently the big toe joint - podagra, ankles, and knees), causing intense acute pain, swelling, and redness.

### High-Purine Foods to Strictly Eliminate
* **Meats & Seafood:** Red meat (mutton, lamb, beef), organ meats (liver, kidneys), shellfish (prawns, crab), sardines, mackerel.
* **Alcohol:** Beer is the worst trigger because it contains high purines and yeast metabolites while dehydrating the body. Whiskey and rum also impair kidney excretion of uric acid.
* **High Fructose Corn Syrup & Sugary Beverages:** Fructose metabolism in the liver directly consumes ATP and produces uric acid as a byproduct.

### Moderate Purine Foods (Consume in Moderation)
* Dried beans (rajma, chole, lobia), lentils/dals (consume thin dal instead of very thick concentrated dal), spinach, mushrooms, and cauliflower. These plant purines are far less dangerous than meat purines, but should not be consumed in excessive quantities during an active gout flare.

### Foods & Habits That Lower Uric Acid
* **Ample Hydration (3 to 4 Liters Daily):** Drinking abundant water is the single most effective way to flush excess uric acid through the kidneys into urine.
* **Low-Fat Dairy:** Low-fat cow's milk and fresh low-fat curd (dahi) have a uricosuric effect, helping the kidneys clear uric acid.
* **Vitamin C & Lemon Water:** Drinking fresh lemon water (without sugar) alkalizes urine slightly and supports renal excretion of urate.
* **Cherries & Berries:** Contain anthocyanins with natural anti-inflammatory properties that reduce gout flare frequency.

## 3. Fatty Liver (NAFLD - Grade 1 and Grade 2) Reversal

### Is Fatty Liver Reversible?
* **Yes! Grade 1 and Grade 2 Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease are completely reversible.**
* The liver has a remarkable capacity to regenerate once the metabolic overload of excess triglycerides is removed.

### Primary Drivers of Fatty Liver
* Contrary to popular belief, fatty liver is driven more by **excess refined carbohydrates, table sugar, and high fructose** than by dietary fat alone.
* When the liver receives more glucose and fructose than it can burn, it converts the excess into liver triglycerides via *de novo lipogenesis*.

### Key Dietary Steps to Reverse Fatty Liver
1. **Zero Liquid Calories:** Eliminate commercial sodas, sweetened fruit juices, milkshakes, and sugary tea/coffee completely.
2. **Eliminate Deep-Fried Snacks:** Samosas, pakoras, puri, kachori, and namkeen overload the liver with oxidized fats.
3. **Black Coffee (Protective):** Drinking 1 to 2 cups of unsweetened black coffee daily has been proven in clinical studies to reduce liver stiffness, lower liver enzymes (SGPT/ALT), and protect against fibrosis.
4. **Include Omega-3 & Antioxidants:** Add 1 tablespoon of roasted flaxseeds (alsi), 4-5 soaked walnuts, and green leafy vegetables.
5. **Physical Exercise:** 30 minutes of daily brisk walking (at least 150 minutes per week) reduces liver fat even before significant body weight is lost.

## 4. High Blood Pressure & Hypertension (Indian DASH Diet)

### Sodium / Salt Management
* **Strict Daily Limit:** Less than **5 grams of salt per day** (which is exactly **1 level teaspoon**) across all meals, curries, and dals combined.
* **Hidden High-Sodium Traps in Indian Kitchens:**
  * Papad (even roasted papad contains sodium bicarbonate and papad khar).
  * Traditional mango, lime, or chili achar (pickles preserved in high salt).
  * Commercial spice mixes (Chaat masala, pav bhaji masala, Maggi tastemakers).
  * Processed savory snacks (bhujia, salted peanuts, chips, mixture).

### Potassium-Rich Foods to Counteract Sodium
* Potassium encourages the kidneys to excrete sodium and relaxes blood vessel walls.
* **Healthy Sources:** Bananas, fresh tender coconut water (unless suffering from chronic kidney disease or high potassium), spinach, bottle gourd, tomatoes, and drumsticks (moringa).

## 5. High Cholesterol & Triglycerides (Dyslipidemia)

### Triglycerides vs. LDL Cholesterol
* **High Triglycerides (> 150 mg/dL):** Typically driven by excess carbohydrate intake, sugar, refined flour, alcohol, and lack of exercise.
* **High LDL ('Bad') Cholesterol (> 100 mg/dL):** Driven by saturated fats, trans-fats, and genetic liver cholesterol regulation.

### Indian Cooking Oil Guidelines
* **Recommended Oils:** Cold-pressed Mustard oil (sarson), Sesame (til) oil, Groundnut oil, or Olive oil used in moderation.
* **Quantity Limit:** Limit total oil consumption to **no more than 3 to 4 teaspoons (15-20 mL) per person per day** (approximately 500 mL per person per month).
* **Strictly Prohibited:**
  * **Re-used Cooking Oil:** Reheating and frying in the same oil multiple times creates toxic trans-fatty acids and lipid peroxides that rapidly clog coronary arteries.
  * Vanaspati (Dalda), hydrogenated bakery shortening, and unrefined palm oil.

## 6. Kidney Stones (Calcium Oxalate Calculi) Prevention

### Prevention Guidelines
* **The 3-Liter Hydration Rule:** Maintain dilute, pale urine by drinking at least 3 to 3.5 liters of clean water distributed evenly throughout the day and night.
* **Oxalate Restriction:** If stones are diagnosed as Calcium Oxalate, restrict high-oxalate foods: excessive raw spinach (palak), beetroot, tomato seeds, chocolate, and strong black tea.
* **DO NOT Stop Dietary Calcium:** A very common mistake is cutting out all milk or curd. Dietary calcium is essential because it binds to oxalate in the intestines so it is excreted in stools rather than absorbed into the kidneys!
