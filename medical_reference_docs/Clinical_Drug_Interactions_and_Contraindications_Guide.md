# Clinical Drug-Drug, Drug-Food Interactions and Absolute Contraindications Guide
**Authority / Standard:** WHO, CDSCO, British National Formulary (BNF), and American College of Clinical Pharmacy (ACCP)
**Scope:** Outpatient, Primary Care, and Emergency Pharmacovigilance Reference Standards

---

## 1. High-Alert Drug-Drug Interactions (Life-Threatening / Severe Toxicity)

### 1.1 Statins + Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (Macrolides & Azole Antifungals)
- **Interacting Agents:**
  - Statins metabolized via CYP3A4: Atorvastatin, Simvastatin, Lovastatin. (Note: Rosuvastatin and Pravastatin have minimal CYP3A4 metabolism).
  - Interacting Inhibitors: Clarithromycin, Erythromycin (Macrolides); Itraconazole, Ketoconazole, Voriconazole (Azole antifungals); Protease inhibitors (Ritonavir).
- **Clinical Mechanism:** Potent inhibition of cytochrome P450 3A4 enzyme significantly increases systemic plasma statin concentrations (up to 5- to 10-fold).
- **Adverse Outcome:** Severe statin-induced myopathy, acute rhabdomyolysis, marked CPK elevation (>10x ULN), acute renal failure secondary to myoglobinuria.
- **Clinical Management:**
  - Temporarily withhold Atorvastatin or Simvastatin during the short course of Clarithromycin, Erythromycin, or Itraconazole.
  - If statin therapy cannot be paused, substitute with Rosuvastatin (low dose <= 10 mg) or Pravastatin, or switch antibiotic to Azithromycin (minimal CYP3A4 inhibition).

---

### 1.2 ACE Inhibitors / ARBs + Potassium-Sparing Diuretics / Potassium Supplements / NSAIDs (The "Triple Whammy")
- **Interacting Agents:**
  - ACEi / ARBs: Ramipril, Enalapril, Lisinopril, Telmisartan, Losartan, Olmesartan.
  - Potassium-sparing agents: Spironolactone, Eplerenone, Amiloride, Triamterene.
  - Potassium supplements: Potassium chloride syrup/tablets (K-Cit, Potcl).
  - NSAIDs: Diclofenac, Aceclofenac, Ibuprofen, Naproxen.
- **Clinical Mechanism:**
  - ACEi/ARBs dilate efferent renal arterioles; NSAIDs block prostaglandins, causing afferent arteriolar constriction -> precipitous drop in glomerular filtration rate (GFR).
  - Reduced aldosterone secretion + direct potassium retention causes rapid serum potassium accumulation.
- **Adverse Outcome:** Severe life-threatening hyperkalemia (serum K+ > 6.0 mEq/L) causing peaked T waves, conduction blocks, fatal ventricular arrhythmias, and acute oliguric renal failure.
- **Clinical Management:**
  - Never prescribe high-dose NSAIDs to patients stabilized on an ACEi/ARB + Diuretic. For analgesia, use Paracetamol (up to 3g/day) or topical formulations.
  - Monitor serum potassium and creatinine within 1-2 weeks of initiating or adjusting ACEi/ARB or Spironolactone therapy.

---

### 1.3 Antiplatelets / Anticoagulants + NSAIDs / SSRIs (Major Hemorrhagic Crisis)
- **Interacting Agents:**
  - Anticoagulants: Warfarin, Acenocoumarol (Acitrom), Direct Oral Anticoagulants / DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran), Heparin / LMWH.
  - Antiplatelets: Aspirin (Ecosprin), Clopidogrel (Clopilet, Deplatt), Ticagrelor (Brilinta), Prasugrel.
  - Co-prescribed agents: NSAIDs (Diclofenac, Aceclofenac, Ibuprofen, Nimesulide); SSRIs (Escitalopram, Sertraline, Fluoxetine).
- **Clinical Mechanism:**
  - NSAIDs cause direct gastric mucosal injury, inhibit platelet COX-1, and impair platelet aggregation.
  - SSRIs deplete platelet serotonin stores, reducing hemostatic plug formation.
  - Concurrent anticoagulation prevents fibrin clot stabilization.
- **Adverse Outcome:** 3- to 6-fold increased risk of upper gastrointestinal bleeding, hematemesis, melena, hemorrhagic stroke, and surgical site hematoma.
- **Clinical Management:**
  - Avoid systemic NSAIDs in any patient on anticoagulation or dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT).
  - If anti-inflammatory relief is mandatory, co-prescribe a prophylactic Proton Pump Inhibitor (Pantoprazole 40 mg or Esomeprazole 40 mg daily) and limit NSAID duration to < 3-5 days.

---

### 1.4 Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) Inhibitors + Nitrates (Fatal Refractory Hypotension)
- **Interacting Agents:**
  - PDE-5 Inhibitors: Sildenafil (Manforce, Revatio), Tadalafil (Megalis, Cialis), Vardenafil.
  - Organic Nitrates: Nitroglycerin (sublingual, transdermal, IV), Isosorbide dinitrate (Sorbitrate), Isosorbide mononitrate (Monotrate, Ismo).
- **Clinical Mechanism:**
  - Nitrates generate nitric oxide, stimulating cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) production.
  - PDE-5 inhibitors block the degradation of cGMP.
  - Dual action causes massive, uncontrolled systemic vasodilation.
- **Adverse Outcome:** Catastrophic profound hypotension, cardiovascular collapse, myocardial infarction, and death.
- **Absolute Contraindication:**
  - Co-administration is strictly contraindicated.
  - Withhold Nitrates for at least 24 hours after Sildenafil administration, and at least 48 hours after Tadalafil administration before giving rescue nitrate therapy.

---

### 1.5 Serotonergic Combinations (Serotonin Syndrome)
- **Interacting Agents:**
  - SSRIs / SNRIs: Escitalopram, Sertraline, Fluoxetine, Paroxetine, Venlafaxine, Duloxetine.
  - Analgesics: Tramadol, Tapentadol, Pethidine, Fentanyl.
  - Antimicrobials: Linezolid (possesses non-selective MAO inhibitory action).
  - Triptans / Antimigraine: Sumatriptan, Zolmitriptan.
  - Other: Dextromethorphan (high dose cough syrups), St. John's Wort.
- **Clinical Mechanism:** Excessive agonism of central and peripheral 5-HT1A and 5-HT2A receptors.
- **Adverse Outcome:** Serotonin Syndrome triad:
  1. Neuromuscular hyperactivity: Clonus (spontaneous, inducible, ocular), hyperreflexia, muscle rigidity, tremor.
  2. Autonomic hyperactivity: Hyperthermia (>38.5°C in severe cases), tachycardia, labile blood pressure, diaphoresis, shivering.
  3. Mental status changes: Agitation, delirium, confusion, hypomania.
- **Clinical Management:**
  - Avoid combining Tramadol with SSRIs/SNRIs. Use alternative analgesics (Paracetamol, topical agents).
  - Check Linezolid prescription carefully; discontinue serotonergic agents at least 2 weeks prior (5 weeks for Fluoxetine) unless emergency monitoring is available.

---

### 1.6 Metformin + Iodinated Radiographic Contrast Media
- **Interacting Agents:** Metformin (Glycomet) and IV/arterial iodinated contrast used in CT scans or cardiac catheterization.
- **Clinical Mechanism:** Contrast-induced nephropathy leads to acute decline in renal function, precipitating toxic accumulation of Metformin.
- **Adverse Outcome:** Severe Metformin-Associated Lactic Acidosis (MALA), with mortality rates exceeding 30-50%.
- **Clinical Management Protocol:**
  - If eGFR is 30-60 mL/min/1.73m² or intra-arterial contrast is administered: Discontinue Metformin at the time of or prior to the imaging procedure.
  - Withhold Metformin for 48 hours following the procedure.
  - Re-evaluate renal function (serum creatinine / eGFR); only resume Metformin if renal function is stable.
  - If eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m², Metformin is absolutely contraindicated.

---

### 1.7 Methotrexate + NSAIDs / Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (Cotrimoxazole)
- **Interacting Agents:** Methotrexate (Folitrax) + NSAIDs (Diclofenac, Ibuprofen, Naproxen) or Cotrimoxazole (Bactrim, Septra).
- **Clinical Mechanism:**
  - NSAIDs reduce renal blood flow and compete for organic anion transporter (OAT1/OAT3) secretion of Methotrexate.
  - Cotrimoxazole displaces Methotrexate from albumin and causes additive folate antagonism.
- **Adverse Outcome:** Severe acute Methotrexate toxicity: Life-threatening pancytopenia, severe mucositis, gastrointestinal ulceration, and hepatic/renal necrosis.
- **Clinical Management:** Avoid NSAIDs on days of weekly Methotrexate dosing. Cotrimoxazole is contraindicated with high-dose or moderate-dose Methotrexate.

## 2. Crucial Food-Drug & Beverage Interactions

### 2.1 Dairy Products / Calcium / Iron / Antacids + Fluoroquinolones & Tetracyclines
- **Medicines Affected:**
  - Fluoroquinolones: Ciprofloxacin (Ciplox), Levofloxacin (Levomac), Ofloxacin (Zenflox), Norfloxacin.
  - Tetracyclines: Doxycycline (Doxy-1), Minocycline (Minoz).
- **Interacting Elements:** Milk, yogurt, cheese, calcium-fortified juices, antacids containing aluminium/magnesium (Digene, Gelusil), oral iron supplements (Orofer XT, Dexorange), sucralfate.
- **Mechanism:** Formation of insoluble multivalent cation chelates in the gut lumen, reducing antibiotic gastrointestinal absorption by up to 75-90%.
- **Outcome:** Clinical treatment failure, persistent bacterial infection, and emergence of antimicrobial resistance.
- **Administration Rule:** Take fluoroquinolones and doxycycline at least **2 hours before or 4 hours after** consuming dairy products, antacids, or mineral supplements.

---

### 2.2 Levothyroxine (Thyronorm, Eltroxin) + Food / Coffee / Minerals
- **Interacting Elements:** Breakfast, milk, tea, coffee, dietary fiber, calcium supplements (Shelcal), iron tablets (Orofer XT), antacids.
- **Mechanism:** Adsorption and chelation in the stomach significantly impairs oral absorption of levothyroxine.
- **Outcome:** Persistent elevated TSH, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, fluctuating thyroid levels.
- **Administration Rule:**
  - Must be taken **first thing in the morning on an empty stomach with a full glass of plain water**.
  - Wait at least **30 to 60 minutes** before consuming tea, coffee, breakfast, or other medications.
  - Separate calcium, iron, or antacid supplements by at least **4 hours** from Levothyroxine.

---

### 2.3 Alcohol + Metronidazole / Tinidazole (Disulfiram-Like Reaction)
- **Interacting Agents:** Metronidazole (Metrogyl, Flagyl), Tinidazole (Fasigyn), Cefoperazone.
- **Mechanism:** Inhibition of hepatic aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH), leading to toxic accumulation of acetaldehyde following alcohol ingestion.
- **Symptoms:** Severe throbbing headache, facial flushing, copious vomiting, abdominal cramps, diaphoresis, chest pain, palpitations, and tachycardia.
- **Rule:** Strict avoidance of all alcoholic beverages and alcohol-containing cough syrups/mouthwashes during therapy and for **at least 48-72 hours after completion** of the antibiotic course.

---

### 2.4 Grapefruit Juice + CYP3A4-Substrate Medications
- **Interacting Medications:** Atorvastatin, Simvastatin, Amlodipine, Felodipine, Cyclosporine, Carbamazepine.
- **Mechanism:** Irreversible inhibition of intestinal CYP3A4 enzymes by furanocoumarins in grapefruit juice, leading to elevated systemic drug bioavailability.
- **Outcome:** Exaggerated hypotension, profound peripheral edema, or statin toxicity.
- **Rule:** Avoid large quantities of whole grapefruit or grapefruit juice while taking these medications.

## 3. High-Risk Drug Class Contraindications Matrix

| Drug Class | Absolute Clinical Contraindications | Key Reason / Adverse Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NSAIDs (Diclofenac, Aceclofenac, Ibuprofen)** | Active peptic ulcer, third trimester of pregnancy, severe heart failure (NYHA III-IV), severe renal failure (eGFR <30), post-CABG surgery | Massive GI bleed, premature closure of fetal ductus arteriosus, acute renal failure, cardiovascular thrombosis |
| **ACE Inhibitors & ARBs (Ramipril, Telmisartan)** | Pregnancy (all trimesters), history of angioedema, bilateral renal artery stenosis, concurrent aliskiren in diabetes | Teratogenic (fetal renal agenesis, oligohydramnios, skull hypoplasia), acute renal shutdown, fatal asphyxia from airway angioedema |
| **Non-Selective Beta-Blockers (Propranolol)** | Severe bronchial asthma, active bronchospasm, second/third-degree AV block without pacemaker, cardiogenic shock | Life-threatening bronchoconstriction, fatal bradycardia or asystole |
| **SGLT2 Inhibitors (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin)** | eGFR < 20-25 mL/min (glycemic failure), type 1 diabetes mellitus without specialist protocol, active diabetic ketoacidosis | Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis (euDKA), severe mycotic genital infections, Fournier gangrene |
| **Proton Pump Inhibitors (Pantoprazole, Omeprazole)** | Known severe hypersensitivity to benzimidazoles; caution in long-term unmonitored use | Hypomagnesemia, vitamin B12 malabsorption, increased fracture risk, Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea |
| **Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin)** | History of tendon rupture/tendinitis, myasthenia gravis, known QT prolongation / congenital long QT | Tendon rupture (Achilles tendon), exacerbation of myasthenia muscle weakness, Torsades de Pointes fatal arrhythmia |
| **Combined Oral Contraceptives (Estrogen + Progestin)** | Active or past history of DVT/PE, ischemic heart disease, stroke, active breast cancer, migraine with aura, women age >35 who smoke | 5- to 10-fold increased risk of venous thromboembolism, ischemic stroke, and myocardial infarction |
| **Methotrexate** | Pregnancy and breastfeeding, severe hepatic dysfunction, severe bone marrow depression / pre-existing blood dyscrasias | Potent embryotoxicity/teratogenicity (fetal death/malformations), fatal pancytopenia, acute liver failure |
