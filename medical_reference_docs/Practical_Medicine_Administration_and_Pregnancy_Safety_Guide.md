## 1. Ophthalmic & ENT Administration Techniques

### Eye Drops Proper Administration & The "5-Minute Spacing Rule"
* **Correct Technique:**
  1. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water.
  2. Tilt head backward, look up at the ceiling, and gently pull down the lower eyelid with clean fingertips to create a small "pocket".
  3. Squeeze exactly 1 drop into the pocket. Do not let the dropper tip touch your eye, eyelashes, or fingers.
  4. Gently close your eyes for 2 minutes. **Do NOT blink rapidly or squeeze eyelids tightly**, as this squeezes the drop out of the eye into the tear duct.
  5. **Punctal Occlusion:** Gently press the inner corner of your eye (near the bridge of your nose) with a clean finger for 1 to 2 minutes. This prevents the medication from draining into your tear duct, throat, and bloodstream, eliminating the bitter medicine taste in your mouth and preventing systemic side effects.
* **THE CRITICAL 5-MINUTE SPACING RULE:**
  * If your doctor has prescribed two or more different eye drops (e.g. an antibiotic like Moxifloxacin and an artificial tear lubricant like Carboxymethylcellulose):
  * **ALWAYS WAIT AT LEAST 5 MINUTES BETWEEN DROPS.**
  * The human eye pocket can hold only one drop of liquid at a time (approx. 25-30 microliters). If you instill the second drop immediately after the first, the second drop simply overflows and washes out the first drop, leaving you with zero therapeutic benefit from the first medicine.
  * Always apply eye drops first, and eye ointments last (ointment forms a protective film that prevents liquid drops from penetrating).

### Ear Drops Proper Administration
* **Temperature Warning:** Never instill cold ear drops straight out of the refrigerator, as cold fluid hitting the eardrum stimulates the inner ear vestibular system, causing immediate severe dizziness and nausea (caloric reflex). Warm the bottle between your palms for 1 to 2 minutes first.
* **Technique:** Lie down on your side with the affected ear facing up. Instill the prescribed number of drops into the ear canal. **Remain lying on your side for 3 to 5 minutes** so gravity draws the medication deep into the eardrum. Do not shove dry cotton tightly into the canal, as cotton absorbs the medication away from the ear tissue.

### Inhalers, Rotahalers & MDI (Metered Dose Inhalers)
* **Technique:** Exhale completely away from the device. Place the mouthpiece between lips with a tight seal. Press down on the canister while taking a slow, deep, smooth breath in over 5 seconds. Hold breath for 10 seconds to allow drug particles to settle in the lungs.
* **THE CRITICAL STEROID MOUTH RINSE:**
  * If your inhaler contains a steroid (Budesonide, Fluticasone, Beclomethasone, Ciclesonide):
  * **ALWAYS rinse your mouth thoroughly and gargle with plain water, then spit it out.**
  * Leaving steroid residue in the mouth suppresses local oral immunity, causing fungal oral thrush (white patches on tongue, burning sensation) and hoarseness of voice (dysphonia).

### Nasal Sprays (Decongestants vs. Anti-Allergy Steroids)
* **Xylometazoline & Oxymetazoline (Otrivin, Nasivion):**
  * **STRICT 3-TO-5 DAY LIMIT:** These are powerful vasoconstrictors that give rapid relief from nasal congestion. However, using them for more than 5 consecutive days damages the nasal vascular regulation, causing **Rhinitis Medicamentosa (rebound congestion)** where the nose becomes permanently swollen and blocked without the spray.
* **Steroid Nasal Sprays (Fluticasone, Mometasone):**
  * Point the spray nozzle slightly outward toward the ear of the same side, **away from the center nasal septum**. Spraying directly onto the nasal septum causes crusting and nosebleeds (epistaxis).

## 2. Empty Stomach vs. After Meals: The Clinical Rationale

### Why PPI Antacids MUST Be Taken Before Meals
* **Medications:** Pantoprazole (Pantocid), Omeprazole (Omez), Rabeprazole (Razo), Esomeprazole (Nexpro).
* **Instruction:** Take **30 to 45 minutes BEFORE breakfast** with a full glass of water.
* **The Reason:** PPIs work by inhibiting active hydrogen-potassium ATPase enzymes ('proton pumps') in the stomach wall. The largest number of proton pumps are recruited and activated when you eat your first meal of the day. If taken after eating, the acid has already flooded the stomach and the medicine's effectiveness is reduced by up to 50%.

### Why Painkillers (NSAIDs) MUST Be Taken After Food
* **Medications:** Paracetamol, Aceclofenac (Hifenac), Diclofenac (Voveran), Ibuprofen (Brufen), Naproxen.
* **Instruction:** Always take **after food or with milk**. Never take on a completely empty stomach.
* **The Reason:** NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandin synthesis in the stomach lining. Prostaglandins produce the natural protective mucus layer that prevents stomach acid from digesting the stomach wall itself. Taking painkillers on an empty stomach causes acute gastritis, severe burning pain, and can cause stomach ulcers or bleeding.

## 3. Pregnancy & Breastfeeding Safety Guide

### Safe Medications in Pregnancy (Under Doctor Guidance)
* **Fever & Mild Pain:** **Paracetamol** is the analgesic of choice and is considered safe across all three trimesters of pregnancy at standard doses (500mg to 650mg).
* **Antibiotics (When prescribed for bacterial infections):** Amoxicillin, Amoxicillin-Clavulanic acid, Azithromycin, Cephalexin, and Cefixime have strong safety records in pregnancy.
* **Allergies & Sneezing:** Cetirizine, Levocetirizine, and Chlorpheniramine are safe when needed.
* **Acidity & Heartburn:** Sucralfate, Famotidine, and Calcium/Magnesium antacids.

### STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in Pregnancy
1. **NSAID Painkillers (Ibuprofen, Aceclofenac, Diclofenac, Naproxen):**
   * **STRICTLY FORBIDDEN IN THE 3RD TRIMESTER (after week 28):** NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandins, which can cause premature closure of the fetal *ductus arteriosus* (a vital fetal blood vessel), leading to pulmonary hypertension in the newborn, as well as fetal kidney dysfunction and low amniotic fluid (oligohydramnios).
2. **Doxycycline & Tetracycline:**
   * Binds to calcium in developing teeth and bones, leading to permanent brownish-yellow discoloration of the child's teeth and impaired bone development.
3. **Isotretinoin & Retinoids (Acne treatments):**
   * Severe teratogen. Causes severe skull, brain, and cardiovascular birth defects. Must be avoided with double contraception.
4. **Blood Pressure Medicines (ACE Inhibitors & ARBs):**
   * **Telmisartan, Losartan, Enalapril, Ramipril:** Strongly contraindicated throughout pregnancy. Causes fetal renal failure, skull hypoplasia, and fetal death. Pregnant women with high BP are managed with safe alternatives like Labetalol or Methyldopa.
5. **Cholesterol Statins (Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin):**
   * Cholesterol is essential for fetal organ and nervous system development; statins are contraindicated.
