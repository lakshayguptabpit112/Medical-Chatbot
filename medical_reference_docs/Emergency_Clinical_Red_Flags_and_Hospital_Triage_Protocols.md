# Emergency Clinical Red Flags, Critical Danger Signs and Hospital Triage Protocols
**Authority / Standards:** American Heart Association (AHA), American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP), WHO IMCI, and Surviving Sepsis Campaign
**Scope:** Immediate Clinical Triage, Emergency Department Referral, and Acute Resuscitation Directives

---

## 1. Acute Chest Pain and Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)

### 1.1 High-Risk Ischemic Chest Pain Features
- **Characteristics:** Crushing, retrosternal heaviness, squeezing pressure ("an elephant sitting on the chest"), persisting for > 15-20 minutes, not relieved by rest or sublingual nitroglycerin.
- **Radiation:** Classical radiation to the left shoulder, left arm, neck, jaw, interscapular region, or epigastrium.
- **Associated Autonomic Symptoms:** Profuse cold diaphoresis (sweating), dyspnea, nausea, lightheadedness, impending sense of doom.
- **Atypical Presentations ("Silent" MI):** Women, diabetic patients, and elderly individuals frequently present WITHOUT classical chest pain. Instead, they present with isolated unexplained breathlessness, epigastric discomfort, sudden profound fatigue, syncope, or acute confusion.

### 1.2 Life-Threatening Differential Diagnoses ("The Big 5")
1. **Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI / Unstable Angina):** Continuous retrosternal heaviness with ST elevation/depression or elevated cardiac troponins.
2. **Aortic Dissection:** Sudden, tearing or ripping chest pain radiating directly through to the mid-back between the scapulae; pulse deficits or blood pressure asymmetry (> 20 mmHg between arms); new aortic regurgitation murmur.
3. **Pulmonary Embolism (PE):** Sudden pleuritic (sharp, stabbing) chest pain aggravated by deep inspiration, sudden dyspnea, tachypnea, tachycardia, hemoptysis, and unilateral calf swelling (DVT).
4. **Tension Pneumothorax:** Sudden unilateral pleuritic pain, acute dyspnea, absent breath sounds on affected side, hyperresonance, and tracheal deviation away from affected side.
5. **Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave Syndrome):** Severe retrosternal chest pain following forceful vomiting, subcutaneous emphysema (crepitus on chest/neck palpation).

### 1.3 Immediate First-Aid & Emergency Action Plan
- Call local emergency medical services (Ambulance 108 / 102 / 911) immediately.
- Patient must rest in a comfortable semi-recumbent position (head elevated 30-45 degrees). Strict physical exertion restriction.
- **Chewable Soluble Aspirin:** Administer **300 to 325 mg of non-enteric coated Aspirin (Disprin / Ecosprin)** chewed immediately (unless known severe aspirin allergy or active gastrointestinal bleeding). Chewing produces antiplatelet inhibition within 15 minutes.
- Sublingual Nitroglycerin (0.4 mg) may be given if systolic BP is > 100 mmHg and the patient has NOT taken PDE-5 inhibitors (Sildenafil/Tadalafil) in the past 24-48 hours.
- Perform a **12-lead ECG within 10 minutes** of hospital arrival ("Door-to-ECG time").

## 2. Acute Stroke and Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)

### 2.1 The FAST Protocol & Cincinnati Stroke Scale
- **F - Face Drooping:** Ask the patient to smile or show their teeth. Does one side of the face droop or look uneven?
- **A - Arm Weakness:** Ask the patient to raise both arms horizontally with palms up and eyes closed for 10 seconds. Does one arm drift downward or cannot be lifted?
- **S - Speech Difficulty:** Ask the patient to repeat a simple sentence (e.g. "The sky is blue in the morning"). Is speech slurred, garbled, inappropriate, or is the patient unable to speak (aphasia)?
- **T - Time to Call Emergency:** If ANY ONE of these signs is present, the probability of an acute ischemic stroke is > 72%. Note the exact **Time of Symptom Onset** ("last known well" time) and transport immediately to a comprehensive stroke center.

### 2.2 Critical Prehospital and Emergency Directives
- **Thrombolytic Window:** Intravenous tissue plasminogen activator (IV Alteplase / Tenecteplase) must be initiated within **4.5 hours** of symptom onset; mechanical thrombectomy for large vessel occlusions within **6 to 24 hours**.
- **Crucial Rule:** **NEVER administer oral aspirin, antiplatelets, or anticoagulants at home or in an ambulance before a non-contrast CT brain scan has definitively ruled out hemorrhagic stroke.**
- **Blood Pressure Management:** In acute ischemic stroke candidates for thrombolysis, maintain BP < 185/110 mmHg. In non-thrombolysis patients, allow permissive hypertension up to 220/120 mmHg to maintain penumbral brain perfusion. Avoid aggressive acute BP lowering!
- Check fingerstick blood glucose immediately to rule out acute hypoglycemia (which mimics acute focal stroke deficits).

## 3. Acute Neurological and Headache Red Flags (The SNOOP4 Criteria)

### 3.1 Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Brain Imaging (CT/MRI)
- **S - Systemic Symptoms / Signs:** Fever, chills, night sweats, unexplained weight loss, known active malignancy, or immunocompromised state (HIV, chemotherapy).
- **N - Neurological Deficits / Signs:** Confusion, altered mental status, seizures, focal motor/sensory deficits, papilledema, asymmetric pupils, cranial nerve palsies.
- **O - Onset Sudden ("Thunderclap Headache"):** Severe headache reaching maximum peak intensity within **< 1 minute** ("worst headache of my life") -> Diagnostic for **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)** from a ruptured cerebral aneurysm until proven otherwise.
- **O - Older Age at Onset:** New-onset headache starting in patients aged **> 50 years** -> Strongly suspect **Giant Cell (Temporal) Arteritis** (check ESR/CRP; risk of irreversible blindness) or intracranial mass lesion.
- **P - Pattern Change or Progression:** Sudden change in frequency, severity, or clinical character of chronic headaches.
- **P - Postural / Positional Aggravation:** Worsens significantly when standing (intracranial hypotension / CSF leak) or worsens when lying flat or coughing/straining (raised intracranial pressure / cerebral venous sinus thrombosis).

## 4. Acute Abdomen and Surgical Emergencies

### 4.1 Signs of Peritoneal Irritation (Mandatory Surgical Consult)
- **Involuntary Guarding:** Reflex abdominal wall muscle spasm on palpation that cannot be voluntarily relaxed.
- **Board-Like Rigidity:** Abdominal wall muscles are rock hard across all quadrants (indicates widespread generalized peritonitis, often from perforated peptic ulcer or ruptured viscus).
- **Rebound Tenderness (Blumberg Sign):** Sudden sharp lancinating pain upon rapidly withdrawing palpating hands from the abdomen.
- **Silent Abdomen:** Complete absence of bowel sounds over 3-5 minutes (paralytic ileus secondary to severe intra-abdominal sepsis).

### 4.2 High-Risk Presentations
- **Acute Appendicitis:** Periumbilical visceral pain migrating to the Right Lower Quadrant (McBurney's point tenderness, Rovsing sign, Psoas sign), accompanied by low-grade fever and anorexia.
- **Acute Cholecystitis:** Right Upper Quadrant pain radiating to right infrascapular area, fever, leukocytosis, and positive **Murphy's Sign** (abrupt inspiratory arrest on deep RUQ palpation).
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Severe epigastric pain radiating directly to the mid-back, partially relieved by leaning forward, associated with repeated vomiting; serum amylase/lipase elevated > 3x ULN.
- **Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy:** Sudden severe unilateral lower quadrant pelvic pain in any woman of childbearing age, associated with missed period, vaginal spotting, shoulder tip pain (diaphragmatic blood irritation), and hemorrhagic shock.

## 5. Pediatric Danger Signs (WHO IMCI Clinical Guidelines)

### 5.1 The Six Universal IMCI Danger Signs (Immediate Hospitalization)
Any infant or child with ANY of the following signs has a life-threatening illness requiring urgent tertiary hospital care:
1. **Inability to Drink or Breastfeed:** Child is too weak or breathless to suckle or swallow liquids.
2. **Vomiting Everything:** Inability to retain any oral fluids or medications whatsoever.
3. **Convulsions / Seizures:** Active seizure, or history of fits during the current febrile illness.
4. **Lethargy or Unconsciousness:** Child is abnormally sleepy, does not respond to mother's voice, does not make eye contact, or is comatose.
5. **Stridor in a Calm Child at Rest:** Harsh, high-pitched inspiratory sound indicating critical upper airway obstruction (croup, foreign body aspiration, epiglottitis).
6. **Severe Chest Indrawing:** The lower chest wall goes inward (retracts) when the child breathes in, indicating severe pneumonia, bronchiolitis, or respiratory failure.

### 5.2 Pediatric Normal Respiratory Rates (Threshold for Tachypnea)
- Under 2 months of age: Fast breathing is **>= 60 breaths/minute**.
- 2 to 11 months of age: Fast breathing is **>= 50 breaths/minute**.
- 12 months to 5 years: Fast breathing is **>= 40 breaths/minute**.

## 6. Severe Sepsis and Septic Shock (The qSOFA Criteria)

### 6.1 Quick Sepsis-Related Organ Failure Assessment (qSOFA)
A patient with suspected infection who scores **>= 2 points** is at high risk of in-hospital mortality and requires immediate ICU/HDU sepsis resuscitation:
- **Respiratory Rate:** **>= 22 breaths/minute** (1 point).
- **Altered Mental Status:** Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) **< 15** (lethargy, drowsiness, acute confusion, disorientation) (1 point).
- **Systolic Blood Pressure:** **<= 100 mmHg** (1 point).

### 6.2 The Sepsis Resuscitation Bundle
1. Measure blood lactate level immediately (lactate > 2 mmol/L indicates tissue hypoperfusion; > 4 mmol/L indicates severe metabolic crisis).
2. Obtain blood cultures prior to antibiotic administration.
3. Administer broad-spectrum IV antimicrobials within the first hour ("Golden Hour").
4. Rapidly infuse **30 mL/kg of IV crystalloids** (Ringer's Lactate or Normal Saline) for hypotension or lactate >= 4 mmol/L.
5. Apply vasopressors (Norepinephrine first choice) if patient remains hypotensive during or after fluid loading to maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) >= 65 mmHg.

## 7. Anaphylaxis Emergency Management Protocol

### 7.1 Diagnostic Criteria
Acute onset (minutes to hours) of an illness involving skin/mucosal tissue (hives, pruritus, flushed lips/tongue/uvula swelling) AND AT LEAST ONE of:
- **Respiratory Compromise:** Dyspnea, wheeze, bronchospasm, stridor, reduced peak expiratory flow.
- **Cardiovascular Collapse:** Hypotension (SBP < 90 mmHg or > 30% drop from baseline), syncope, urinary/fecal incontinence.
- **Severe Crampy Abdominal Pain and Recurrent Vomiting** following exposure to a known allergen.

### 7.2 Immediate Pharmacological Protocol
- **First-Line Drug of Choice:** **INTRAMUSCULAR (IM) EPINEPHRINE (ADRENALINE 1:1,000 / 1 mg/mL)**.
  - Dose: **0.5 mg (0.5 mL)** for adults and children > 12 years; **0.3 mg (0.3 mL)** for children 6-12 years; **0.15 mg (0.15 mL)** for children under 6 years.
  - Injection Site: **Mid-anterolateral aspect of the outer thigh** (vastus lateralis muscle). Rapid absorption within 5 minutes.
  - Repeat every **5 to 15 minutes** if symptoms fail to resolve or progress.
- **Positioning:** Lay patient flat on their back with legs elevated (Trendelenburg/shock position). Do NOT allow the patient to stand up or sit upright suddenly (causes empty heart syndrome and sudden cardiac arrest).
- **Oxygen & IV Access:** High-flow oxygen (10-15 L/min via non-rebreather mask) + rapid IV fluid bolus.
- **Adjunctive Second-Line Agents (Do NOT delay Epinephrine):**
  - IV/IM Antihistamine: Chlorpheniramine 10 mg or Diphenhydramine 50 mg.
  - IV Corticosteroid: Hydrocortisone 200 mg or Dexamethasone 8 mg (prevents biphasic rebound anaphylaxis 4-8 hours later).
  - Nebulized Salbutamol (2.5 - 5 mg) for persistent bronchospasm.
