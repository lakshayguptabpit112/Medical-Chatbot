# Patient Guide to Diagnostic Scans, Imaging & Laboratory Preparation

**Document Type:** Patient Diagnostic Guidance & Test Preparation Manual  
**Domain:** diagnostic_imaging_and_tests  
**Target Audience:** Patients, Family Members & Healthcare Consumers  
**Clinical Standards:** ICMR, AIIMS & AERB Aligned  

---

## 1. Ultrasound (USG / Sonography) Scans & Patient Preparation

### USG KUB & PVR (Kidneys, Ureters, Bladder & Post-Void Residual)
* **What This Test Is:** A non-invasive soundwave scan imaging the urinary tract: kidneys, ureters, and bladder.
* **Why Doctors Order It:**
  * Lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS): increased urinary frequency, painful urination (dysuria), waking up at night to urinate (nocturia).
  * Suspected kidney stones (renal calculi), burning sensation, or urinary tract infection (UTI).
  * Difficulty passing urine or weak stream in men (suspected benign prostatic hyperplasia - BPH).
* **What "PVR" Means (Post-Void Residual):**
  * The sonologist measures how much urine is left in the bladder *immediately after* the patient urinates.
  * **Normal PVR:** Less than 50 mL.
  * **Elevated PVR (> 100 mL):** Indicates urinary retention, bladder muscle weakness, or bladder outlet obstruction (such as an enlarged prostate in men).
* **Patient Preparation Instructions:**
  * **Full Bladder Required:** Drink 4 to 5 glasses of plain water (around 1 liter) 1 hour before the scan and DO NOT urinate until the doctor scans your full bladder.
  * Once the full bladder scan is complete, you will be asked to completely empty your bladder in the washroom, and a second quick scan is done to measure the residual urine (PVR).
  * Fasting is generally NOT required for KUB alone.

### USG Whole Abdomen & Pelvis
* **What It Evaluates:** Liver, Gallbladder, Pancreas, Spleen, Kidneys, Urinary Bladder, and Pelvic Organs (Uterus/Ovaries in females, Prostate in males).
* **Why Doctors Order It:** Abdominal pain, bloating, suspected gallstones, fatty liver, jaundice, or pelvic cramps.
* **Patient Preparation Instructions:**
  * **Strict Fasting (6 to 8 hours):** The gallbladder must remain distended with bile to detect small gallstones or wall thickening. Eating causes the gallbladder to contract, hiding stones.
  * Plain water is permitted, but avoid milk, tea, coffee, juice, or greasy food before the scan.
  * In the final 1 hour before the scan, drink 3 to 4 glasses of water to fill the urinary bladder for the pelvic evaluation.

### Obstetric Ultrasound (Pregnancy Scans)
* **Dating / Viability Scan (6-9 weeks):** Confirms intrauterine pregnancy, heartbeat, and gestational age.
* **NT Scan (Nuchal Translucency, 11-13 weeks):** Screens for chromosomal conditions like Down Syndrome.
* **TIFFA / Anomaly Scan (18-20 weeks):** Detailed head-to-toe structural check of the baby's brain, heart, spine, kidneys, and limbs.
* **Patient Preparation:** Wear loose, comfortable two-piece clothing. Early scans (first trimester) usually require a moderately full bladder.

## 2. Heart & Cardiovascular Diagnostics

### Electrocardiogram (ECG / EKG)
* **What It Measures:** The electrical activity, rhythm, and conduction signals of the heart via small adhesive electrodes placed on the chest, arms, and legs.
* **Why Doctors Order It:** Chest pain, palpitation, breathlessness, irregular heartbeat, baseline check before surgery, or high blood pressure evaluation.
* **Patient Preparation:** Painless, non-invasive, takes only 3 to 5 minutes. No fasting required. Avoid wearing metal jewelry or tight clothing.

### 2D Echocardiogram (2D Echo) with Color Doppler
* **What It Measures:** A dedicated ultrasound of the heart muscles, chambers, and heart valves in real-time.
* **Key Findings Explained:**
  * **Ejection Fraction (EF):** Percentage of blood leaving the heart each time it contracts. Normal EF is **55% to 70%**. An EF below 40% indicates heart failure or reduced pumping efficiency.
  * **Valvular Status:** Checks for valve leakage (regurgitation) or narrowing (stenosis) in Mitral, Aortic, or Tricuspid valves.
  * **Wall Motion:** Checks if any heart muscle area is weakened or damaged from a past silent heart attack.
* **Patient Preparation:** Completely painless and non-invasive. No fasting required.

### TMT (Treadmill Test / Cardiac Stress Test)
* **What It Evaluates:** How well the heart handles physical exercise and whether heart arteries have blockages under stress.
* **Patient Preparation:**
  * Wear comfortable walking or running shoes and light clothing.
  * Have a light meal 2 to 3 hours prior; avoid a heavy meal, tea, coffee, or smoking.
  * Check with your doctor if any blood pressure medicines (especially Beta-blockers like Metoprolol or Atenolol) should be withheld on the morning of the test.

## 3. Radiography & Advanced Imaging (X-Ray, CT, MRI)

### Chest X-Ray (PA View - Postero-Anterior)
* **What It Evaluates:** Lungs, heart shadow size, rib cage, diaphragm, and pleural spaces.
* **Why Doctors Order It:** Persistent cough (>2 weeks), fever, breathlessness, suspected pneumonia, tuberculosis screening, or rib injury.
* **Patient Preparation:** Remove necklaces, chest piercings, and clothing with metal zippers or buttons. The radiographer will ask you to take a deep breath and hold it for 2 seconds while the image is captured.

### Computed Tomography (CT Scan) - Plain vs. Contrast (CECT)
* **Plain CT (Non-Contrast):** Quick cross-sectional X-ray slices. Used for acute trauma, brain hemorrhage, stroke evaluation, and kidney stone localization. No contrast dye needed.
* **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT):** An iodine-based intravenous contrast dye is injected to highlight blood vessels, tumors, infections, and abdominal organs.
* **CRITICAL Patient Preparation for CECT:**
  * **Serum Creatinine Test Required:** You MUST have a recent normal Serum Creatinine report. The kidneys excrete the contrast dye; if kidneys are impaired, contrast can cause nephropathy.
  * **Fasting:** 4 to 6 hours fasting before contrast administration to prevent nausea or vomiting.
  * **Allergy Check:** Inform the radiology team if you have ever had an allergic reaction to seafood, iodine, or previous X-ray dye.

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
* **What It Evaluates:** Superior soft-tissue contrast for the brain, spinal cord, disc herniation (sciatica), knee ligaments (ACL/meniscus), and joints.
* **CRITICAL Safety Warning:**
  * MRI uses an extremely powerful magnetic field.
  * **STRICTLY FORBIDDEN:** Patients with older cardiac pacemakers, metallic aneurysm clips, cochlear implants, or metal fragments in the eye CANNOT enter an MRI room.
  * Modern titanium dental implants and joint replacements are usually MRI-safe, but always inform the technician beforehand.

## 4. Blood & Specimen Collection Patient Instructions

### Fasting Blood Glucose (FBS) & Lipid Profile
* **Fasting Duration:** Requires **10 to 12 hours of overnight fasting** for Lipid Profile, and **8 to 10 hours** for Fasting Blood Sugar.
* **What is Permitted:** Plain drinking water is allowed and encouraged to keep veins hydrated for smooth blood drawing.
* **What is Strictly Forbidden:** Tea, coffee (even without sugar), milk, juice, morning snacks, biscuits, or chewing gum.
* **Special Rule for Lipid Profile:** Avoid heavy fried feasts, fatty meats, or alcohol the night before the test, as this falsely spikes triglyceride numbers.

### Post-Prandial Blood Sugar (PPBS)
* **Timing:** The blood sample must be drawn **EXACTLY 2 hours after the start of your main meal** (from the first bite of breakfast or lunch), not 2 hours after finishing.
* Take your prescribed diabetes medications as scheduled with that meal unless your doctor instructed otherwise.

### Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c)
* **No Fasting Required:** Unlike daily glucose tests, HbA1c measures glucose attached to red blood cells over the past 90 days. It can be drawn at any time of day, regardless of meals.

### Urine Routine & Microscopy (Urine R/M) & Urine Culture
* **Mid-Stream Clean Catch (MSU) Technique:**
  1. Wash hands thoroughly and clean the genital area with plain water.
  2. Start urinating into the toilet for the first 1-2 seconds (this flushes out normal bacteria from the urethral opening).
  3. Without stopping the stream, place the sterile specimen container into the middle part of the urine stream to collect 20-30 mL.
  4. Finish urinating into the toilet. Seal the container immediately without touching the inside of the rim.
