# Patient Practical Medicine Administration, Food Timings, and Missed Dose Management Guide
**Standard Authorities:** Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC), NHS Medicines Information, CDC, and American Pharmacists Association (APhA)
**Scope:** Outpatient Medication Safety, Device Administration Technique, and Patient Counseling Protocols

---

## 1. Accurate Meal Timing Terminology for Oral Medications

### 1.1 "Empty Stomach" (Before Food / Khali Pet)
- **Scientific Definition:** Take medication at least **1 hour before a meal** OR at least **2 hours after completing a meal**.
- **Rationale:** The stomach is physically empty, gastric acid is at baseline, and gastric emptying is rapid. Co-ingestion with food would bind to the medicine, delay absorption, or degrade the active chemical molecule in digestive juices.
- **Key Medications Requiring Empty Stomach:**
  - **Levothyroxine (Thyronorm, Eltroxin):** Take first thing in the morning with plain water; wait 30-60 minutes before breakfast, tea, or coffee.
  - **Proton Pump Inhibitors (Pantoprazole, Rabeprazole, Esomeprazole, Omeprazole):** Take 30-60 minutes before breakfast.
  - **Antitubercular Rifampicin / Isoniazid:** Food reduces absorption by > 30%. Take 1 hour before breakfast.
  - **Bisphosphonates (Alendronate, Risedronate):** Take first thing in morning with full glass of plain water; remain sitting upright for 30 minutes without eating or drinking anything else.
  - **Sucralfate (Sucrafil):** Take 1 hour before meals and at bedtime to coat ulcer craters prior to food-stimulated acid secretion.

### 1.2 "With Food / Immediately After Meals" (After Food / Khane Ke Baad)
- **Scientific Definition:** Ingest the medication during the meal or within **10 to 15 minutes after finishing the meal**.
- **Rationale:**
  1. Prevents direct gastric mucosal irritation, nausea, gastritis, and ulcers.
  2. Enhanced absorption: Dietary fats are required to dissolve and absorb lipophilic medications.
- **Key Medications Requiring Post-Meal Administration:**
  - **NSAIDs & Analgesics (Diclofenac, Aceclofenac, Ibuprofen, Combiflam, Zerodol-SP, Nimesulide):** Never take on an empty stomach to prevent acute gastric erosions and ulcers.
  - **Metformin (Glycomet):** Take during or immediately after meals to minimize common gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, metallic taste, diarrhea).
  - **Oral Iron Supplements (Orofer XT, Dexorange):** Best absorbed on empty stomach with Vitamin C, but commonly taken with/after meals if nausea, cramps, or gastric irritation occur.
  - **Antifungals (Itraconazole - Canditral, Sporanox):** Take immediately after a full fatty meal or with an acidic beverage (e.g. cola/lemon juice) to maximize gastric dissolution.
  - **Steroids (Prednisolone, Deflazacort, Medrol):** Take immediately after breakfast to mitigate ulcer risk and align with natural morning cortisol spikes.

### 1.3 "Bedtime" (At Night / Raat Ko Sone Se Pehle)
- **Rationale:** Aligns with circadian metabolic rhythms, nocturnal symptom peaks, or allows sedative effects to assist sleep.
- **Key Medications:**
  - **Statins (Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin, Simvastatin):** Hepatic HMG-CoA reductase cholesterol synthesis peaks during physiological fasting at night (midnight to 4 AM).
  - **Sedating Antihistamines (Cetirizine, Levocetirizine, Hydroxyzine):** Mitigates daytime drowsiness.
  - **Antihypertensives with Postural Side Effects (Prazosin, Doxazosin):** Prevents "first-dose syncope" and postural orthostatic hypotension.
  - **Urological Alpha-Blockers (Tamsulosin, Silodosin):** Take 30 minutes after the evening meal to reduce daytime lightheadedness.

## 2. Universal Missed Dose Clinical Decision Protocol

### 2.1 The Golden General Rule
- **The Rule:** If you forget a scheduled dose, take it **as soon as you remember**.
- **The Exception:** If it is almost time for your next scheduled dose (i.e. more than halfway through the dosing interval), **skip the missed dose entirely** and take your next dose at the regular scheduled time.
- **Universal Warning:** **NEVER take two doses at the same time or take an extra dose to make up for a forgotten pill.** Doubling doses can cause sudden toxic overdose, profound hypotension, fatal hypoglycemia, or internal hemorrhage.

### 2.2 Class-Specific Missed Dose Action Matrix

| Medication Category | Specific Clinical Instructions on Missed Dose | Critical Patient Advice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Blood Pressure Pills (Telmisartan, Amlodipine, Ramipril)** | Take when remembered on the same day. If remembered the next morning, skip yesterday's missed pill and take today's single tablet. | Do not double up. Check blood pressure if experiencing headache or dizziness. |
| **Oral Diabetes Pills (Metformin, Glimepiride, Vildagliptin)** | If taking with meals, take as soon as remembered with a meal or snack. If remembered hours later near the next meal, skip the missed dose. | **Never take sulfonylureas (Glimepiride) without food** - high risk of severe hypoglycemia. |
| **Insulin Injections (Basal e.g. Lantus vs Prandial e.g. Novorapid)** | - **Basal (Lantus / Tresiba):** Take when remembered. If remembered next day, take usual dose and consult physician.<br>- **Prandial (Rapid-acting with food):** If missed before meal, take immediately after meal. If hours later, skip and check blood glucose. | Check blood glucose every 2-4 hours. Have fast-acting sugar (glucose tablets/fruit juice) available. |
| **Antibiotics (Amoxicillin, Cefuroxime, Azithromycin)** | Take as soon as remembered. If within 2 hours of the next dose, skip and resume regular schedule. | **Always complete the full prescribed course**, even if symptoms disappear, to prevent drug resistance. |
| **Blood Thinners (Warfarin, Acitrom)** | Take as soon as remembered that same calendar day. If not remembered until the next day, skip and take normal dose. | Keep a calendar log. Report unexplained bruising, bleeding gums, dark tarry stools, or red urine immediately. |
| **DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban)** | - **Twice-daily (Apixaban 5mg):** Take within 6 hours. If > 6 hours elapsed, skip.<br>- **Once-daily (Rivaroxaban 20mg):** Take immediately with food. May take 2 tablets together ONLY if on 15mg twice-daily loading phase. | Maintain regular dosing. Unscheduled omissions increase stroke risk in atrial fibrillation. |
| **Thyroid (Levothyroxine / Thyronorm)** | If missed in the morning, take 2 hours after a meal on an empty stomach. If completely missed for the day, **you may safely take 2 tablets the following morning**. | Levothyroxine has a long half-life (7 days); doubling the single missed dose next morning is safe. |
| **Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCs)** | - **1 pill missed:** Take missed pill immediately (even if it means 2 pills in one day), then continue pack.<br>- **2 pills missed in week 1 or 2:** Take 2 pills today and 2 pills tomorrow, then resume 1 daily. **Use barrier contraception (condoms) for 7 days.** | If unprotected intercourse occurred in the past 5 days and 2+ pills were missed in Week 1, consider emergency contraception. |

## 3. Inhaler and Respiratory Device Proper Technique Guide

### 3.1 Metered Dose Inhaler (MDI) with Spacer (The Gold Standard)
1. **Preparation:** Remove mouthpiece cover. Shake inhaler vigorously up and down for 5 seconds.
2. **Assembly:** Insert the inhaler canister nozzle firmly into the rubber end of the spacer chamber.
3. **Exhalation:** Sit upright. Breathe out gently and completely away from the spacer to empty the lungs.
4. **Placement:** Put the spacer mouthpiece between your teeth and close your lips tightly around it to create an airtight seal (do not bite or block with tongue).
5. **Actuation & Inhalation:** Depress the canister ONCE to release a puff into the spacer chamber. Breathe in **slowly and deeply** through your mouth over 3 to 5 seconds. (If the spacer makes a whistling sound, you are breathing in too fast).
6. **Breath-Hold:** Remove mouthpiece from mouth. Hold your breath for **10 seconds** (or as long as comfortable) to allow medicine droplets to settle deep into the bronchial tree.
7. **Second Puff:** If your doctor prescribed 2 puffs, wait **60 seconds** before repeating the process. Never actuate two puffs into the spacer at the same time.

### 3.2 Crucial Post-Steroid Gargling and Mouth Rinsing
- **Applies to:** All Inhaled Corticosteroids (Budesonide, Fluticasone, Beclomethasone, Foracort, Seroflo, Budecort).
- **Mandatory Procedure:** Immediately after completing your inhaler dose, **rinse your mouth thoroughly with plain water, gargle deep in the throat, and SPIT THE WATER OUT**. Do not swallow the rinse water.
- **Why this is critical:** Deposited steroid particles on the pharyngeal and tongue mucosa suppress local immunity, leading to **Oral Thrush (Candidiasis - white painful patches on tongue/palate)** and vocal cord myopathy causing **Dysphonia (chronic hoarseness of voice)**.

## 4. Insulin Storage, Temperature & Injection Site Rotation

### 4.1 Temperature and Storage Rules
- **Unopened Vials and Cartridges:** Store in a refrigerator between **2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F)** until the expiration date. Store on a refrigerator shelf, **NEVER in the freezer or near the chiller compartment**. Frozen insulin breaks its protein structure and is permanently ruined.
- **Opened / In-Use Insulin Pen or Vial:** May be kept at **room temperature (below 30°C / 86°F)** in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and heat for up to **28 to 30 days** (up to 42-56 days for Insulin Degludec/Tresiba).
- **Comfort Tip:** Injecting cold insulin directly from the refrigerator causes localized stinging and discomfort. Allow cold pens/vials to warm to room temperature for 15-20 minutes prior to injection.

### 4.2 Injection Site Rotation to Prevent Lipohypertrophy
- **Recommended Anatomical Sites:**
  1. **Abdomen:** At least 2 inches (5 cm) away from the umbilicus/belly button (fastest and most predictable absorption).
  2. **Anterolateral Thigh:** Outer upper thigh (intermediate absorption).
  3. **Upper Buttocks / Hips:** (Slowest absorption, ideal for long-acting basal insulin).
- **Site Rotation Method:** Rotate injection spots systematically within the chosen anatomical zone (e.g. moving clockwise by 1-2 cm for each injection).
- **Pathology Prevented:** Repeated injections in the exact same spot cause **Lipohypertrophy** (rubbery, thickened subcutaneous fat lumps). Injecting into hypertrophied tissue impairs insulin absorption, leading to erratic, unexplained blood glucose spikes and unexpected severe hypoglycemia.

## 5. Ophthalmic (Eye) and Otic (Ear) Drops Administration

### 5.1 Eye Drops Application Protocol
1. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water.
2. Tilt head backward and look upward toward the ceiling.
3. Gently pull down the lower eyelid with a clean index finger to form a small pocket ("conjunctival sac").
4. Hold the dropper directly over the eye without touching the tip to the eye, eyelashes, or skin (prevents bottle contamination).
5. Squeeze the bottle to release **exactly 1 drop** into the conjunctival pocket. (The human eye can hold only 20-30 microliters; a second drop immediately overflows and is wasted).
6. Close the eye gently for **2 minutes**. Do NOT blink repeatedly or squeeze the eyes shut.
7. **Nasolacrimal Occlusion:** Press your index finger gently against the inner corner of your eye (next to the bridge of the nose) for 1 to 2 minutes. This blocks the tear duct, preventing the drop from draining into the nasal mucosa and bloodstream, thereby eliminating systemic side effects and foul taste in the throat.
8. **Multiple Eye Drops Interval:** If using more than one eye medication, **wait 5 to 10 minutes** before instilling the second drop. If using eye drops AND eye ointment, always apply the drops first and the ointment last.
