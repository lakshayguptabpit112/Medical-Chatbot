# Outpatient Chronic Disease Clinical Protocols and Evidence-Based Treatment Targets
**Standard Authorities:** ACC/AHA, ADA Standards of Care, GINA, GOLD, RSSDI, and Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)
**Scope:** Primary Care Outpatient Clinical Decision Support and Long-Term Chronic Care

---

## 1. Essential Hypertension Management

### 1.1 Blood Pressure Classification (ACC/AHA & ICMR)
- **Normal:** Systolic < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
- **Elevated:** Systolic 120-129 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg (Lifestyle modification indicated).
- **Stage 1 Hypertension:** Systolic 130-139 mmHg OR Diastolic 80-89 mmHg.
- **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Systolic >= 140 mmHg OR Diastolic >= 90 mmHg.
- **Hypertensive Crisis:** Systolic > 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic > 120 mmHg (Requires immediate emergency department evaluation for target organ damage).

### 1.2 Target Blood Pressure Goals
- **Standard Outpatient Target:** **< 130/80 mmHg** for all adult patients with confirmed hypertension, including those with diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease (CKD), or established coronary artery disease.
- **Elderly (Age >= 65 years, ambulatory):** Systolic target **< 130 mmHg** (tailored to frailty, postural dizziness, and fall risk).

### 1.3 Stepwise First-Line Pharmacotherapy (The A-C-D Protocol)
- **First-Line Drug Classes:**
  1. **A:** ACE Inhibitors (Ramipril 2.5-10 mg, Enalapril 5-20 mg) OR ARBs (Telmisartan 40-80 mg, Losartan 50-100 mg).
  2. **C:** Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine 5-10 mg, Cilnidipine 10-20 mg).
  3. **D:** Thiazide / Thiazide-like Diuretics (Chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg, Indapamide 1.5 mg, Hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-25 mg).
- **Monotherapy vs Dual Therapy Initiation:**
  - If baseline BP is **> 20/10 mmHg above target** (e.g. Stage 2 >= 140/90 mmHg), initiate therapy with **two first-line agents of different classes** (preferred: ARB + CCB like Telma-AM, or ARB + Thiazide-like diuretic like Telpres-CT).
  - Cilnidipine is preferred over Amlodipine in patients experiencing dependent pedal edema, sympathetic reflex tachycardia, or microalbuminuria.
- **Resistant Hypertension:** Uncontrolled BP despite full adherence to optimal doses of three antihypertensive agents of different classes (one of which is a diuretic). Step 4: Add Spironolactone (12.5 to 25 mg daily) provided serum potassium is < 4.5 mEq/L and eGFR > 45 mL/min.

## 2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Management

### 2.1 Diagnostic Criteria (ADA & RSSDI)
- **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) after at least 8 hours fasting.
- **2-Hour Post-Load Glucose (75g OGTT):** >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L).
- **Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c):** >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) in a standardized laboratory assay.
- **Random Plasma Glucose:** >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss).

### 2.2 Standard Glycemic Control Targets
- **HbA1c Target:** **< 7.0%** (53 mmol/mol) for most non-pregnant adults to minimize microvascular and macrovascular complications.
  - More stringent target (< 6.5%) for younger patients with short disease duration, long life expectancy, and no significant hypoglycemia risk.
  - Less stringent target (< 8.0%) for patients with history of severe hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy, extensive comorbid conditions, or advanced micro/macrovascular complications.
- **Preprandial Capillary Blood Glucose:** **80 - 130 mg/dL** (4.4 - 7.2 mmol/L).
- **Peak Postprandial Capillary Blood Glucose (1-2 hours after meal start):** **< 180 mg/dL** (10.0 mmol/L).

### 2.3 Stepwise Pharmacotherapy Algorithm
- **First-Line Foundation:** Comprehensive lifestyle modification (dietary deficit 500-750 kcal/day, 150 min/week moderate aerobic exercise) + **Metformin** (titrated from 500 mg daily to 1000 mg twice daily with meals to avoid gastrointestinal upset).
- **Compelling Comorbidity Add-On Indications (Regardless of Baseline HbA1c):**
  - **Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD) or High Risk:** Add GLP-1 Receptor Agonist (Semaglutide, Liraglutide, Dulaglutide) OR SGLT2 Inhibitor (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin) with proven cardiovascular benefit.
  - **Heart Failure (HFrEF or HFpEF):** Add SGLT2 Inhibitor (Dapagliflozin 10 mg or Empagliflozin 10 mg daily) - proven to reduce hospitalizations and cardiovascular mortality.
  - **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD with eGFR 20-60 or persistent albuminuria):** Add SGLT2 Inhibitor (Dapagliflozin or Empagliflozin) to halt renal progression; add GLP-1 RA if SGLT2i is not tolerated.
  - **Need for Weight Loss:** Prioritize GLP-1 RA (Semaglutide) or dual GIP/GLP-1 RA, or SGLT2i.
  - **Need to Minimize Hypoglycemia:** DPP-4 Inhibitor (Vildagliptin, Sitagliptin, Linagliptin), SGLT2 Inhibitor, or GLP-1 RA. Avoid Sulfonylureas (Glimepiride, Glibenclamide) if hypoglycemia is hazardous.

### 2.4 Acute Hypoglycemia Management (The "Rule of 15")
- **Definition:** Blood glucose < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L).
- **Protocol:**
  1. Consume **15 grams of fast-acting simple carbohydrates** (e.g. 3-4 glucose tablets, 1/2 cup / 120 mL fruit juice or regular non-diet soda, 1 tablespoon sugar or honey dissolved in water). Avoid chocolate or milk as fat delays glucose absorption.
  2. Wait **15 minutes** at rest.
  3. Re-test blood glucose.
  4. If still < 70 mg/dL, repeat with another 15 grams of carbohydrates.
  5. Once blood glucose is >= 70 mg/dL, consume a meal or complex carbohydrate snack with protein (e.g. roti, sandwich, crackers) to prevent recurrent hypoglycemia.

## 3. Dyslipidemia & Cardiovascular Risk Reduction

### 3.1 Lipid Reference Standard and Treatment Targets (ACC/AHA & National Lipid Association)
- **Total Cholesterol:** Desirable < 200 mg/dL.
- **Triglycerides:** Normal < 150 mg/dL; Borderline 150-199 mg/dL; High 200-499 mg/dL; Very high >= 500 mg/dL (risk of acute pancreatitis).
- **HDL-Cholesterol:** Normal >= 40 mg/dL in men; >= 50 mg/dL in women.
- **LDL-Cholesterol Goals Based on ASCVD Risk:**
  - **Extreme / Very High Risk (Established ASCVD, prior MI, stroke, or Diabetes with target organ damage):** **LDL-C < 55 mg/dL** (1.4 mmol/L) AND >= 50% reduction from baseline.
  - **High Risk (Diabetes without ASCVD, severe CKD, or LDL-C >= 190 mg/dL):** **LDL-C < 70 mg/dL** (1.8 mmol/L).
  - **Moderate / Low Risk:** **LDL-C < 100 mg/dL**.

### 3.2 Statin Intensity Tiers
- **High-Intensity Statin Therapy (Lowers LDL-C by >= 50%):**
  - Atorvastatin 40 to 80 mg daily.
  - Rosuvastatin 20 to 40 mg daily.
- **Moderate-Intensity Statin Therapy (Lowers LDL-C by 30% to 49%):**
  - Atorvastatin 10 to 20 mg daily.
  - Rosuvastatin 5 to 10 mg daily.
  - Simvastatin 20 to 40 mg daily.
- **Non-Statin Add-On Therapy:** If LDL-C remains above target despite maximum tolerated statin dose:
  - Add **Ezetimibe 10 mg daily** (provides an additional 15-20% LDL reduction).
- **Severe Hypertriglyceridemia (Triglycerides >= 500 mg/dL):** Initiate Fenofibrate (145-200 mg daily) or Saroglitazar (4 mg daily) to prevent acute pancreatitis; eliminate refined sugars and alcohol.

## 4. Hypothyroidism Outpatient Management

### 4.1 TSH Target Ranges (Indian Thyroid Society & ATA)
- **General Non-Pregnant Adult Target:** **TSH 0.4 to 4.0 mIU/L** with normal Free T4 (FT4: 0.8 - 1.8 ng/dL).
- **Elderly (> 65-70 years) or Cardiac Disease:** Relaxed TSH target **3.0 to 6.0 mIU/L** to avoid precipitating atrial fibrillation, angina, or osteoporosis from overtreatment.
- **Trimester-Specific Pregnancy TSH Targets:**
  - **First Trimester:** **< 2.5 mIU/L** (or lab-specific lower limit).
  - **Second & Third Trimester:** **< 3.0 mIU/L**.
  - Any woman on Levothyroxine who becomes pregnant must immediately increase her dose by approximately 25-30% (typically 2 extra tablets per week) and contact her endocrinologist/obstetrician.

### 4.2 Levothyroxine Dosing and Titration
- Full replacement dose for overt primary hypothyroidism: ~1.6 mcg/kg/day (ideal body weight).
- In elderly patients or those with coronary artery disease: Start low and go slow (25 to 50 mcg daily).
- Re-check serum TSH **6 to 8 weeks** after initiating therapy or after any dose modification (TSH equilibration takes 6 weeks due to the 7-day half-life of T4).

## 5. Asthma and COPD Outpatient Protocols

### 5.1 GINA Stepwise Asthma Management (2023 Update)
- **Track 1 (Preferred Strategy - SMART / MART):** Low-dose **ICS-Formoterol** (e.g. Budesonide 160 mcg + Formoterol 4.5 mcg, Foracort 200) used as both daily maintenance AND symptom reliever across all severity steps.
  - Rationale: Using SABA (Salbutamol) alone increases asthma exacerbation risk and airway hyperresponsiveness; combining ICS with fast-acting Formoterol treats underlying inflammation immediately during every rescue puff.
- **Track 2 (Alternative Strategy):** SABA (Salbutamol 100 mcg) as needed for rescue, provided patient is taking daily maintenance ICS.
- **Patient Technique Check:** Always verify inhaler technique and mouth-rinsing after every steroid dose to avoid oral candidiasis and dysphonia.

### 5.2 COPD Maintenance Standards (GOLD Guidelines)
- **Group A (Low symptoms, low exacerbation risk):** Bronchodilator (SABA or SAMA as needed).
- **Group B (High symptoms mMRC >=2, low exacerbation risk):** Long-acting bronchodilator: **LABA + LAMA** (e.g. Tiotropium + Formoterol or Glycopyrronium + Indacaterol).
- **Group E (High exacerbation risk >=2 moderate or >=1 hospitalization):** **LABA + LAMA**. If blood eosinophils >= 300 cells/mcL, add Inhaled Corticosteroid (**Triple Therapy: LABA + LAMA + ICS**).
- Absolute smoking cessation counseling and annual Influenza + Pneumococcal vaccination are mandatory in all COPD stages.

## 6. Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD) & Peptic Ulcers

### 6.1 Standard Outpatient PPI Protocol
- **Initial Therapy:** Standard once-daily oral Proton Pump Inhibitor (Pantoprazole 40 mg, Esomeprazole 40 mg, Rabeprazole 20 mg, or Omeprazole 20 mg) for **4 to 8 weeks**.
- **Timing:** Must be ingested **30 to 60 minutes before the first meal of the day (breakfast)**. Acid-activated PPIs require actively secreting H+/K+ ATPase proton pumps to bind effectively.
- **Endoscopy Alarm Features:** Do not treat empirically with PPI without prompt Upper GI Endoscopy if any red flag is present:
  - Progressive dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) or odynophagia (painful swallowing).
  - Unexplained weight loss (>5% in 6 months).
  - Persistent non-responsive vomiting.
  - Evidence of gastrointestinal blood loss (hematemesis, melena, unexplained iron deficiency anemia).
  - New onset dyspepsia in an adult aged > 55 years.

## 7. Gout & Hyperuricemia Management (ACR Standards)

### 7.1 Acute Gout Flare Management
- **First-Line Options (Initiate within 24 hours of flare onset):**
  1. **Colchicine:** 1.0 or 1.2 mg at flare onset, followed by 0.5 or 0.6 mg one hour later (total 1.5-1.8 mg on Day 1), then 0.5 mg once or twice daily until flare resolves. High-dose colchicine regimens cause toxic diarrhea and are obsolete.
  2. **NSAIDs:** Etoricoxib 90-120 mg daily, or Naproxen 500 mg twice daily, or Diclofenac 50 mg three times daily (with food/PPI).
  3. **Oral Corticosteroids:** Prednisolone 30 to 35 mg daily for 5 days (preferred in patients with renal impairment where NSAIDs and Colchicine are contraindicated).
- **Critical Rule:** **Do NOT initiate or discontinue Urate-Lowering Therapy (Allopurinol / Febuxostat) during an acute gout attack.** Rapid fluctuations in serum urate prolong and worsen the acute synovitis. Continue existing dose if already on it.

### 7.2 Chronic Urate-Lowering Therapy (ULT)
- **Indications:** >= 2 gout flares per year, presence of tophi on clinical exam or imaging, or radiographic joint damage attributable to gout.
- **First-Line Agents:** **Allopurinol** (start at 100 mg/day, titrate upward every 2-4 weeks) OR **Febuxostat** (40 to 80 mg daily).
- **Serum Uric Acid Target:** **< 6.0 mg/dL** (360 micromol/L); for patients with tophaceous gout, target **< 5.0 mg/dL** to promote crystal dissolution.
- **Flare Prophylaxis:** When initiating ULT, always co-prescribe low-dose anti-inflammatory prophylaxis (Colchicine 0.5 mg daily or low-dose NSAID) for **at least 3 to 6 months** to prevent mobilization flares.
